Forecasting Net Bike Flow at Montreal's Busiest BIXI Stations

Charts and examples use the 3-hour look-ahead; tables show 1, 3 and 6 hours

00/ Abstract

A bike-share station never shuts down, but it can turn riders away: empty, with no bike to take, or full, with no dock to return one to. We forecast net flow, arrivals minus departures, at Montreal's 100 busiest BIXI stations over the next 1, 3 and 6 hours, with an 80% range. A gradient-boosting model with quantile outputs is trained on summers 2022 and 2023 and tested on every hour of 2024 to 2026, using weather forecasts as they were issued. It is scored on the windows that matter, against hard baselines, as a shortlist for a dispatcher, and, on real station counts from 2025, as a probability that a station will run empty, with both station-count results rerun once on a sealed month from 2024. To decide where trucks should go, how close a station is to empty or full right now matters far more than the size of its forecast; the forecast adds a little on top.

01/ The problem

A station stays open all day; what goes wrong is that it stops being useful. When it is empty, a rider who wants a bike has to walk to another station; when it is full, a rider arriving by bike cannot return it there. Both are caused by imbalance: more bikes leaving than arriving, or the reverse. The quantity forecast is therefore net flow at station s over the h hours that start at hour t:

ys,t(h)=∑k=0h−1(as,t+k−ds,t+k),h∈{1,3,6}
Arrivals a minus departures d, added over the window. Positive fills the station, negative drains it.

On the map: every circle is this number for the selected hour; blue gains, red loses, size is how much.

02/ System overview

One chain of steps turns raw trips into everything the map shows. Each step writes a file the next one reads; the map and this note read only the last one. The three evaluations at the bottom all score the same forecasts.

03/ Data
  • Trips. BIXI's public trip history, January 2022 to August 2026, 57.8 million trips. Counting them per station and hour gives arrivals and departures.
  • Stations. Trip files carry names and coordinates, not stable ids, and stations get renamed and moved. Two records are one station if they are within 25 m, or have the same name within 150 m. The 100 busiest are ranked on summer trips in 2022 and 2023.
  • Weather. ECMWF forecast runs as issued, via Open-Meteo, for one downtown point. A run is used only 8 hours after it started, roughly when it would have been available.
  • Station counts. For sections 11 and 12 only: snapshots of BIXI's live station feed (bikes, free docks, capacity), saved about every 15 minutes by a public archive, May to September 2025, plus September 2024 kept aside as a sealed confirmation.

Summer is May to September. Every input for hour t uses only data from before t.

04/ What counts as right

Net flow is skewed. An average over every window is dominated by the many ordinary ones, and over all windows a forecast of zero misses by . The big windows are where stations empty or fill. So the main score is the average miss on the biggest-change windows: the 10% of station-windows with the largest |net flow|, set per look-ahead.

miss=1|B|∑(s,t)∈B|ys,t−y^s,t|,B={(s,t):|ys,t|≥c}
Average absolute miss, in bikes, over the big windows B. The cut-off c is 9, 18 and 29 bikes at 1, 3 and 6 hours ( in the 3-hour examples).
05/ Baselines

A forecast is worth something only if it beats simple rules. Five are scored exactly like the model:

  • Predict zero: no change. The floor.
  • Just before (persistence): the net flow of the window that just ended. Weak here, because flow reverses: out in the morning, back in the evening.
  • Last week: the same window one week ago (seasonal naive).
  • Last 4 weeks: the same window averaged over four weeks. The hard one.
  • Level 1 ("Simple average" on the map): the average by station, hour of week and temperature band, from 2022 and 2023.
06/ Model

Level 2 ("This model" on the map) is gradient-boosted decision trees: an ensemble built one shallow tree at a time, each fitted to the errors left by the trees before it. One global model per look-ahead is shared by all 100 stations, so quiet stations borrow strength from busy ones. Its inputs:

  • Recent state: net flow, arrivals and departures over the last 1, 3 and 6 hours; trips over the last day and week.
  • Seasonal memory: the same window yesterday, last week, and averaged over 4 weeks.
  • Calendar and place: hour, day of week, weekend, holiday, latitude, longitude.
  • Expected weather over the window: temperature, rain, wind, from the forecast that existed then.

Four models share these inputs: one minimises squared error (the forecast) and three minimise the pinball loss at the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles (the range):

Lq(y,y^)={q(y−y^)ify≥y^(1−q)(y^−y)otherwise
At q = 0.9 a miss below costs nine times a miss above, so the model settles where 90% of outcomes fall under it.

Why gradient boosting, and not a Gaussian process or a neural network

The data is tabular: about 880,000 station-hours per look-ahead in the test years alone, with mixed calendar, weather and lag inputs. Gradient-boosted trees are the standard strong learner for this shape of problem; tree ensembles (LightGBM) dominated the top of the M5 retail forecasting competition. An exact Gaussian process scales with the cube of the number of training points, which rules it out at this size without heavy approximation. Deep sequence models are not guaranteed to win either: a benchmark across transport datasets found a weekly historical average competitive with many deep-learning methods. The comparison that decides the model is the one in section 09, against hard baselines, not the model's complexity.

Tree size was chosen once, by learning on 2022 and checking on 2023, then refitted on both years. Nothing from 2024 onward touched any choice.

What the model needs, and two alternatives

Each row refits the forecast with one change and scores it like section 09, against the model above, with day-block intervals. Removing a group shows how much the model relies on it; the last two rows try obvious improvements: adding what neighbouring stations (within 600 m) just did, and forecasting arrivals and departures separately before subtracting.

07/ Uncertainty

The range runs from the 10th to the 90th percentile model and should hold 80% of outcomes. Fitted on 2022 and 2023, it held slightly less later on. A self-correcting band recalibrates it each day from its own recent record, using only outcomes already known:

mi=max(li−yi,yi−ui)(ui−li)/2,λtoday=Q0.8({mi:last 28 days})
Each past miss m is how far the outcome fell outside its band, in half-band-widths (negative if inside). Both edges move out, or in, by λ half-widths: the stretch that would have made 80% of the last 28 days' bands hold.

On the map: a circle fades when its range crosses zero, meaning the model cannot tell whether the station gains or loses.

08/ Evaluation protocol
  • Forward only. Train on the past, score on the future. Hours are never shuffled.
  • Weather as forecast. Using observed weather would be cheating; scored both ways, honesty costs at most 0.04 bikes.
  • Leakage tests. Automated tests rewrite every hour after t and check that no input for hour t moves.
  • Never retrained. The map's model was fitted once; refitting each year is scored separately below.
  • Pre-registration. For sections 10 to 12, questions, baselines and the rule for "better" were written down before any result was seen.
  • A sealed confirmation. The station-count results (sections 11 and 12) were confirmed on a month kept aside, September 2024: run once, with nothing refitted, under a plan written before its data was downloaded, including what would count as confirmed.

10/ From forecast to decision

A dispatcher needs to know which few stations to check. So the forecast is also scored as a shortlist: at each decision time, take the K = 10 largest forecasts and count how many really had a big window. Every ranking, including random picks, chooses from the same pool: the busiest 100 stations in service at that hour, about 85, not the whole network.

precision@K=picks that were really bigK×decision times
Decision times are every hour at the 1-hour look-ahead and every 3 or 6 hours at the others, so no window counts twice. Rankings see only forecasts.

Intervals by resampling days. Neighbouring hours share weather and events, so rows are not independent. Whole days are redrawn with replacement 2,000 times and the metric recomputed; the middle 95% is the interval. A method is called better only if the interval of the paired difference, on the same redrawn days, excludes zero.

One caution, measured in section 12: a shortlist of the biggest moves is not a list of stations about to run empty. A station with many docks can lose 30 bikes and be fine.

On the map: "Where to look" is this shortlist for the selected hour, with what really happened next to each pick.

11/ From net flow to empty stations

Net flow is not stock: losing 20 bikes is harmless at a station that started with 30. Real station counts for summer 2025, a year inside the test period, allow the real question to be scored. This is a retrospective test on past data.

Start from the bikes there now, b, and treat the change as normal around the forecast μ with spread σ read off the 80% range; then recalibrate with a one-variable logistic regression fitted on May to July 2025:

P(empty)≈Φ(0.5−b−μσ),σ=q0.9−q0.12×1.2816
Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution; 0.5 treats bikes as whole. Full is the mirror image, with free docks in place of bikes.

The model learned from trips, and trips do not include BIXI's trucks moving bikes. Comparing the real change in bikes at each station with the trip count for the same window measures what trips miss, and explains why the raw probability above is too sure.

12/ Which stations to send a truck to

BIXI's dispatchers give each station an acceptable range of bikes, set by hand from experience, and a station outside it raises an alert. There are more alerts than trucks, so stations are ranked mostly by location: first those empty or full whose neighbours within 600 m are too, then those within 600 m of a metro station, then those near either (Silva, Aloise and Jena, 2024, describing the 2019 and 2020 seasons).

Three simple rankings are compared on the real 2025 counts. At each decision time each picks 10 stations, and a pick counts if the station really is empty or full at the end of the window:

  • Forecast size: the largest predicted change, as in section 10.
  • Stock only: the stations closest to empty or full right now. No forecast.
  • Stock + forecast: the chance of ending empty or full, from section 11.
  • Random: 10 stations drawn from the same pool, the busiest stations in service at both ends of the window.

BIXI's own rule is not scored. These counts already include what its trucks did while following that rule, so the stations it sent trucks to look fixed; scoring it here would be unfair. Comparing against it properly needs BIXI's truck logs, which is the natural next step for this project.

13/ Limits
  • Only the 100 busiest stations. Every result, including "better than random", is measured within them. In summer 2025 they carried 25% of trips and 37% of the big 3-hour moves, but only about 9% of the time a station sat empty (7% for full): most empty stations are quiet ones. A forecast is worth most where flow is big (at the other ~800 stations most 3-hour windows move 2 bikes or fewer); a network-wide dispatch list would lean on the stock count, as section 12 found.
  • Trucks are invisible in trips, so trip net flow is not the real change in stock (section 11).
  • Small edges. 1% to 8% over a 4-week average on big windows, about 2 points on the shortlist, 1% to 2.4% on empty stations.
  • No event calendar. The worst misses are festivals and matches.
  • It ages when never retrained (section 09).
  • A changing network. 85 of the 100 stations were in service in 2026.
  • About 90 days of real counts, in two separate periods: August and September 2025 (61 test days, 91 stations) and September 2024 (30 days, 88 stations).
  • No sealed dates for the trip-based results (sections 09 and 10, 2024 to 2026): they were read once and nothing was tuned afterwards.
